# The core: claims composed, not papers decomposed

**Status:** proposed, with a running prototype at `chain/` and a worked round at `chain/examples/scientopia/`
**Revised:** 12 Sep 2026, afternoon — players replace workers; set inputs close the loop; see `docs/scientopia/`
**Frames:** [#31](https://github.com/zmainen/elife-claim-trees/issues/31) · [#56](https://github.com/zmainen/elife-claim-trees/issues/56) · [#82](https://github.com/zmainen/elife-claim-trees/issues/82) · [#104](https://github.com/zmainen/elife-claim-trees/issues/104) · [#34](https://github.com/zmainen/elife-claim-trees/issues/34)
**Reads:** the earlier notes in this directory as evidence of what was tried, not as authority
**Board:** `chain/examples/scientopia/board.html` — every artifact of the demo, generated, no script

The thesis is claims and modularity. This note designs a core in which a claim is the atom of
every artifact, a paper or a proposal or a review is a composition of claims, and the whole
research, publishing and funding cycle is one process over one schema, run by agents and
optionally read by people. It is deliberately smaller than the morning's draft. The claim-tree
work is not ported here; it is the first thing to port once the core has been tightened.

## 1. Claims are the atom, not the subject

The current repository decomposes papers into claims: a paper is the unit of work, and claims
are what the pipeline extracts from it. Inverting that means claims come first and documents
are made from them. Under the inversion:

- **A claim** is a node with a type, a text, who asserted it and when, and typed edges to other
  claims. Its id is global (`alpha:study:r1`), so claims link across projects and across time.
- **A composition** is a document: a proposal, a paper, a published record. It carries no
  claims of its own, only sections that reference claims by id. A paper is claims composed,
  not claims copied.
- **A review** is claims about claims: one `assessment` per item with a verdict from a declared
  vocabulary. **A decision** — funding, editorial, a maintainer's approval of a version — is a
  claim with a verdict about a composition.

So is a claim a *subject*? No. A subject is the scope a process instance runs under — a
project, a manuscript, the corpus, the process itself. Claims are the content of every
artifact any subject produces, and because their ids are global they are not confined to the
subject that produced them. This is what lets the legacy work rejoin later: decomposing a
published paper is an *import* step whose answer is a claim set, landing in the same graph as
claims a project produced itself.

The single general schema, then, is one file shape:

```json
{"claims":   [{"id": "r2", "type": "result", "text": "…", "by": "stub-agent"}],
 "edges":    [{"from": "r2", "to": "alpha:hypotheses:h2", "rel": "contradicts"}],
 "sections": [{"title": "Results", "claims": ["alpha:study:r1", "alpha:study:r2"]}]}
```

Ten claim types (`question hypothesis prediction method result interpretation scope
assessment decision step`), ten relations (`answers derived-from tests supports contradicts
part-of replicates cites assesses decides`). Small on purpose; every term is mapped to
standards in `chain/standards.yaml` (§ 6).

## 2. What the current repository taught

The analysis behind this note read `pipeline/layers.yaml`, `scripts/pipeline.py`, the four
review scripts, the site's badge code and eleven issues. Four things in it are right and are
kept: a declared graph with content-hashed inputs and computed staleness; the separation of the
*question* (`--dump-prompt`) from *who answers it* (`--answer`); approval as an operation on a
version; validators as the contract with whatever answered. Five things are baked in and are
dropped:

- The paper as the hard-wired subject, with corpus scope as a stateless special case (#77).
- A `kind` field mixing who answers, whether outputs accumulate, whether a layer mutates
  another's output, and whether it is a decision. `feature` layers write into `claims/`, so a
  claim file's provenance is the union of five layers' runs and no layer can connect a
  promoted claim (#99).
- Eight incompatible mechanisms for one act, a person recording a judgement:
  `approvals.jsonl`, `mappings/`, `review/gap-claim-decisions.jsonl`, `review/<topic>.json`,
  `review/prediction-outcome.json`, the #82 verdict file, the method's never-built gate, and
  `api/review.py`. The pipeline unified runs and never unified decisions.
- `reads` as a declaration nothing enforces (`audit_layers.py` reports sixteen mismatches).
- Presentation fields (`views`, `how`, `found`) in the process file, and a badge folding three
  facts into one word.

## 3. The core

Five nouns. **Player** (a subject): what a process instance runs against — a team, a funder,
a journal, the library, the process itself — a persona file plus whatever answers its
requests. **Step**: a question asked of a player, with inputs, instructions, and what it may
emit or judge; a step with a command is run by the referee, any other waits for its player.
The core does not say whether a player is a person or an agent; the run records who
answered. **Claim**: the atom (§ 1). **Version**: a step's answer for a player, one immutable
directory. **Run**: the record of one answer, inputs by hash, who, when.

Five verbs.

| Verb | Does |
|:--|:--|
| `ask <player> <step>` | Stage the request: `INSTRUCTIONS.md`, `PERSONA.md`, `in/` with a copy of every input, `CONTRACT.json`, `QUESTION.md`, and for a judging step `skeleton.json`. Ask first whatever the step needs of the same player that is not current. A command runs at once; otherwise the request waits for the player. Idempotent: a current step is not re-asked without `--again`. |
| `answer <subject> <step> <claims.json> --by WHO` | Refuse if any input moved since the request. Validate the claim set. Write `v<N>/` with `run.json`. Append the ledger. |
| `status` | Five states per cell, computed from hashes; two overlays, judgements and scheme (§ 5). |
| `board` | One static page: the process, every version, every open request, the claim graph, the standards, the ledger. |
| `export <subject> <step>` | The version as JSON-LD carrying its PROV record and the mapped IRIs of every claim and relation. |

One rule. **A step reads `$CHAIN_IN` and writes `$CHAIN_OUT`.** The request directory holds
copies of everything the step is entitled to, so what a run could have read and what it did
read are the same list; `reads` is no longer a declaration to police. An answer may refer only
to claims the request staged, so an agent cannot cite what it was not shown, and a step cannot
write into another step's artifact because it has no path to one.

Inputs name a step of the same player, a step of a named player (`library:import`), every
player of a type (`journal:*:published`), or a file. A set input goes stale when a member's
version moves or the membership changes; it never blocks and never orders the graph. That is
how the loop closes — journals publish into the library, every team's reading of the library
is then out of date — without a cycle in the graph and without a wait.

One layout, chosen for git:

```
store/<subject>/<step>/request/     the open question, if any
store/<subject>/<step>/v<N>/        claims.json · run.json · QUESTION.md · INSTRUCTIONS.md
store/<subject>/ledger.jsonl        every run, append-only
```

Every version directory stands alone: it carries the question it answered, the instructions
its answerer was given, and `run.json` naming every input by hash. The whole core is under
900 lines with the board and the export, depends on PyYAML only, calls no model and knows
nothing about papers.

## 4. Agents are black boxes, and their instructions are artifacts

Three things an agent runs on are first-class, hashed into every run, and shown on the board:
the **process specification** (each step's own entry hashes separately, so editing one step
does not stale the rest), the **instructions** (a markdown file per model or human step, staged
into the request as `INSTRUCTIONS.md` and copied into the version it produced), and the
**harness** (the command a code step runs, and the interpreter and package the core puts on
its path). The agent itself is whatever reads the request directory and writes `claims.json`.

The demo's `chain/agents/stub.py` is that black box at its smallest: it asserts the
instructions are present, reads the contract and the skeleton, and fills in a fixture. A real
model adapter, a Claude Code subagent, a batch job or a person replaces it and nothing else in
the system changes, because the request is the whole interface. Agents never address each
other; they meet only as versions in the store. This is a blackboard architecture, and it is
what makes git and Underlay natural substrates (§ 7).

## 5. Human review is a step a person answers

A judging step declares `judges: <step>`. Its request stages the judged artifact and a
skeleton: one `assessment` per item, `unconsidered`, plus one `decision` about the whole. The
items of a step are its claims; the items of a composition are the claims its sections
reference, so a reviewer judges the hypotheses a proposal is made of, not the wrapper. Verdicts
per item come from the step's own vocabulary (`sound / weak / unclear`; `fund / decline`;
`supported / weak / unsupported`; `keep / strike / merge-into`); the overall decision is
`accept`, `reject` or `partial`, and `partial` is required when items are left unconsidered.

Two kinds, by what is judged: an **adjudication** judges a subject's artifact; a **ruling**
judges the process (`judges: process`, one item per step declaration). Same record, same
verbs. Four consequences follow from ordinary staleness rather than from special code:

- A judgement binds to the version it read. When the judged step produces a new version, the
  judgement is stale and `status` says it applied to an earlier version.
- A ruling goes stale when a step's declaration changes; re-asking carries forward every
  verdict whose step is unchanged and asks only about the edited one.
- A gate is an input: `publish` lists `editorial`, so it cannot run until a person has decided.
- The queue of human work is `status`: every `pending` cell whose worker is `human`.

A person's four steps are the same whichever kind: the tool **asks** (stages and pre-fills), the
person **reads** (fills verdicts, stops when they stop), the person **answers** (validated,
refused if the judged version moved meanwhile), and a downstream code step **applies** it.

## 6. Status, badges, and what each fact comes from

| State | Means |
|:--|:--|
| `absent` | never answered |
| `pending` | a request is open and unanswered |
| `current` | answered; every input still as recorded; every upstream current |
| `stale` | an input changed since the answer |
| `blocked` | answered and unchanged, but something upstream is not current |

Two overlays. **Judgements**: for each step that judges this one, who, when, the overall
verdict, the tally per item verdict, and whether it applies — traceable to
`store/<subject>/<judge>/v<N>/claims.json`. **Scheme**: this step's verdict in the newest current
ruling, or `proposed` while the ruling is stale or pending, or `unruled`. The demo, after the
investigator revises the hypotheses, reads:

```
~ proposal   stale  code  v1 · proposal-review: accept by stub-agent (10/10) [an earlier version]
                          · funding: accept by demo-human (10/10) [an earlier version] · scheme: accept
! publish    blocked      ← alpha:paper, alpha:peer-review, alpha:editorial
```

Each clause is one fact from one file, and the board renders the same facts.

## 7. Standards from step zero

`chain/standards.yaml` maps every term the core uses to the standards that already exist, and
says where the fit is poor rather than forcing it. `export` reads it; the board shows it.

| Ours | Maps to | Fit |
|:--|:--|:--|
| run | PROV Activity (`used`, `generated`, `wasAssociatedWith`); in-toto link (materials, products, functionary); DocMaps action | exact, unsigned |
| version | PROV Entity (`wasRevisionOf`, `wasDerivedFrom`); RO-Crate dataset; DocMaps output | exact |
| worker | PROV Person / SoftwareAgent; BPMN userTask / serviceTask; CRediT roles for people | exact |
| process | DocMaps (a subject's ledger is one DocMap); BPMN; CWL for code steps only | good; CWL has no human step |
| claim types | MIRA, Discourse Graphs Q/C/E/S, schema.org | good for question/hypothesis/result; DG has no method or scope node |
| relations | CiTO, MIRA, DG | `tests` deliberately unmapped to CiTO — it is neutral and must not export as support (#37) |
| compositions | OXA article, JATS, schema.org ScholarlyArticle / Review / Grant | good for papers; no shared proposal schema |
| decisions | DocMaps assertion status; schema Review / Grant | no standard node for a decision as such |

The rule is: do not reinvent, map; and where nothing fits, say so in the mapping file so the
gap is visible rather than papered over.

## 8. Git, Underlay, and artifacts as the only channel

Artifacts are small text; version directories are never edited; the ledger is append-only.
Two branches adding runs merge unless they claim the same version number, and then git shows
the conflict on the one directory that matters. A decision committed by its author has the
same name in `by` and in the commit. Underlay fits the same shape for data git should not
carry: a subject is a collection, a version is a collection version, the contract is the
collection schema, `run.json` is its provenance; large outputs live there and the store keeps
their hashes.

## 9. The demonstration: one round of the loop

`chain/examples/scientopia/process.yaml` declares two teams (Juniper, Larch), a funder
(Aurora), a journal (Meridian) and the library, and runs one round: both teams read the
library and propose; Aurora funds Juniper and declines Larch; Juniper's study finds one
prediction met and one failed; both compose papers; Meridian reviews both, publishes
Juniper's and declines Larch's; the library's catalogue is re-asked and now holds the
publication, so every team's reading of the library is out of date. Every answer comes from
the stub, recorded as `stub:<player>`, so the store says plainly that nothing was decided by
anyone. What the demo does not do, on purpose, is any research, review or funding judgement of
substance; the point is the shape, and the loop.

## 10. Porting the claim-tree work, later

The legacy pipeline becomes a process whose subject is a published paper and whose steps are
the readers, reconcile, review and edges, each emitting claims into the same graph; its
`feature` layers become steps producing their own claim sets and `tree` becomes a composition;
its eight review mechanisms become judging steps (`adjudicate-tree` for #82,
`adjudicate-coverage` for `mappings/`, `decide-gaps` for `promote.py`, `rule-relations` for
#19); the corpus vocabulary becomes a step on the `corpus` subject that every model step lists
as an input (closing #77); and the nine March trees become `import` answers by a person. The
site reads `status --json` and the store, and the badge becomes the two overlays (#104).
None of that is started here.

## 11. The tracker

The four labels stand: `kind:code` closes on a PR; `kind:scheme` closes on a ruling version;
`kind:adjudication` closes on a judging step's version; `kind:run` exists only when it costs
money. #31 is this note's mechanism; #19, #20, #28 are rulings; #82 is the first
`adjudicate-tree` answer; #77 and #99 dissolve; #34 is answered by the five verbs.
