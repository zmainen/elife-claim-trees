The process

A step is a question asked of a subject. It reads only what is staged into its request and writes one claim set. code answers itself; model and human steps wait for an answer.

questionprojecthumanWhat is this project asking?
instructions
# Instructions: question

You are the investigator opening a project. State the research question(s) the project exists
to answer, each as one interrogative sentence, and any scope you are setting in advance (what
the project will not attempt). Emit `question` and `scope` claims only. Do not propose answers.
—emits question scope
hypothesesprojectmodelWhat answers does the project bet on, and what would each predict?
instructions
# Instructions: hypotheses

You are given the project's questions. For each, state the answer the project bets on as a
`hypothesis` (one declarative sentence), and for each hypothesis one or more `prediction`
claims: observations that would be made if it were true, concrete enough to be tested by a
study of ordinary size. Link each prediction to its hypothesis with a `derived-from` edge and
each hypothesis to its question with `answers`. Do not describe methods.
questionemits hypothesis prediction
designprojectmodelWhat would be measured, how, and within what limits?
instructions
# Instructions: design

You are given the questions, hypotheses and predictions. Emit `method` claims: what will be
measured, in whom, under what manipulation, with what comparison and what analysis — one claim
per measurement. Emit `scope` claims for what the design cannot decide. Link each method to the
prediction it will test with a `tests` edge. Be conservative: a method that cannot in
practice distinguish the hypothesis from its rivals should be marked so in a scope claim.
question, hypothesesemits method scope

proposal-reviewprojectmodelIs each part of the proposal sound?
instructions
# Instructions: proposal review

You are given a proposal composed of questions, hypotheses, predictions, methods and scope.
Fill the skeleton: one `assessment` per item with a verdict — `sound` (the item is well posed
and the design bears on it), `weak` (posed, but the design or the logic is thin), `unclear`
(you cannot tell what is being claimed) — and one sentence of reasoning in `text`. Then the
`overall` decision: `accept` if you stand behind the whole reading, `partial` if you left items
unconsidered. You are not deciding funding.
proposal, question, hypotheses, designjudges proposal · verdicts sound, weak, unclear
fundingprojecthumanShould this be funded?
instructions
# Instructions: funding

You are the programme officer. You are given the proposal, its review, and the claims it is
composed of. Fill the skeleton: `fund` or `decline` per item is a statement about whether that
part of the work merits support; the `overall` decision is your decision on the proposal.
Write one sentence per verdict. A partial reading is permitted and must be marked `partial`.
proposal, proposal-review, question, hypotheses, designjudges proposal · verdicts fund, decline
studyprojectmodelWhat was found, and what does it mean for the predictions?
instructions
# Instructions: study

You are given the funded design and the predictions it tests. Emit `result` claims — one per
measurement, stating what was observed with its effect size and uncertainty — and
`interpretation` claims saying what the results mean for the hypotheses. Link each result to the
prediction it tested with `tests`, and to the hypothesis with `supports` or `contradicts`.
Report what did not come out. A study whose every prediction succeeded is less believable, not more.
funding, hypotheses, designemits result interpretation

peer-reviewprojectmodelDoes the evidence support each claim the paper makes?
instructions
# Instructions: peer review

You are given the paper and the claims it is composed of. Fill the skeleton: one `assessment`
per claim — `supported` (the evidence shown establishes it), `weak` (consistent with it but
not decisive), `unsupported` (the evidence does not bear on it or points the other way) — with
one sentence saying why. Assess results against their methods and interpretations against
their results. Then the `overall` decision. You do not decide publication.
paper, question, hypotheses, design, studyjudges paper · verdicts supported, weak, unsupported
editorialprojecthumanPublish, revise, or decline?
instructions
# Instructions: editorial decision

You are the editor. You are given the peer review. Record one `decision` about the paper:
`accept`, `reject`, or `partial` if you are returning it for revision with the review standing.
Say in `text` what the decision rests on.
paper, peer-reviewjudges paper · verdicts accept, revise, reject

schemeprocesshumanShould each of these steps exist, and is this what it should do?
instructions
# Instructions: ruling on the process

You are given the process declaration, one item per step. For each step: `accept` if the step
should exist and its question, worker and inputs are what they should be; `reject` otherwise,
saying why. Items whose declaration is unchanged since your last ruling are carried forward and
marked so; you need only read the rest.
—judges process · verdicts accept, reject

Standards

Kept as a mapping from step zero (chain/standards.yaml) and exported with every version. Where a standard does not fit, the note says so.

records

processbpmn: Process. worker code → serviceTask; model → serviceTask with an agent implementation; human → userTask. BPMN has no notion of content-hashed inputs.; cwl: Workflow, for code steps only — CWL has no human or model task and no notion of a pending answer.; docmaps: a DocMap is a manifest of steps with inputs, actions and outputs; our process is the template and a subject's ledger is one DocMap.
stepbpmn: task; cwl: CommandLineTool (code steps); docmaps: step (with `inputs`, `actions`, `assertions`)
runprov: Activity — `prov:used` each input, `prov:generated` the version, `prov:wasAssociatedWith` the answerer; intoto: Link / Statement — materials = `in`, products = `out`, functionary = `by`; unsigned here; docmaps: action, with the answerer as participant
versionprov: Entity, `prov:wasRevisionOf` the previous version, `prov:wasDerivedFrom` each input; ro-crate: a Dataset; the version directory can carry ro-crate-metadata.json as-is; docmaps: output
workerprov: code/model → SoftwareAgent; human → Person; bpmn: serviceTask / userTask; credit: human roles can carry a CRediT term (Investigation, Supervision, …) in `by`
